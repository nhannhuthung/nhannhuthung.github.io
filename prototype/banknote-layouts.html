Banknote Layout Studies

Five ways to lay out a country page, each built with five of your real Canadian notes and the site's own colors and fonts. Everything is clickable: turn cards over, switch sides, open rows. Use the button to compare the light and dark palettes.

My pick: the gallery (B) for the page itself, grouped by the year headings from the timeline (D), with the catalogue sheet (C) opening when you click a card. Countries with many notes, like Viet Nam with 31, get about half as long, and no information is lost.

A

Album page

Front and back sit side by side like a note in a collector's sleeve, with a museum-style label underneath. No slideshow: both sides are always visible.

B

Flip-card gallery

Two notes per row (one on phones). Click a note to turn it over and see the back. The card keeps only the essentials; the full details live one click away.

C

Catalogue sheet

One note at a time, laid out like a catalogue entry: a large photo with a Front / Back switch, and every field in a tidy table. The type is split into two chips: what kind of note it is, and whether it still circulates.

D

Timeline

Notes hang off a rail grouped by year or series, newest at the top. Hover or focus a photo to see its back. The year groups take over the job of the solid and dashed dividers in your layout lines.
